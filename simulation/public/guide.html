The Dining Philosophers Problem

This Operating Systems problem teaches us how multiple processes can safely share limited resources.

1. What is the problem?

Imagine five philosophers sitting around a circular table. There is one fork between every pair of philosophers. Each philosopher needs two forks to eat: the left fork and the right fork.

If every philosopher picks up one fork and waits for another, nobody can eat. This situation is called deadlock.

2. What is a semaphore?

A semaphore is a synchronization mechanism that controls access to shared resources. Think of it as a counter that grants permission to enter a restricted area.

wait() acquires a permit or waits until one is available. signal() releases a resource or returns a permit.

3. Our solution: room = 4

We use a counting semaphore called room, initialized to four, and five binary semaphores, one for each fork.

Only four philosophers may try to acquire forks at a time. This prevents all five philosophers from each holding one fork and waiting forever for the next fork.

4. Algorithm

room = 4
fork[5] = {1, 1, 1, 1, 1}

while (true) {
    think()

    wait(room)
    wait(left fork)
    wait(right fork)

    eat()

    signal(right fork)
    signal(left fork)
    signal(room)
}

5. How does it work?

  1. The philosopher thinks.
  2. The philosopher acquires a room permit.
  3. The philosopher acquires the left and right forks.
  4. The philosopher eats.
  5. The philosopher releases both forks.
  6. The philosopher returns the room permit.

6. Important concepts

Mutual exclusion: A fork cannot be held by two philosophers at the same time.

Deadlock: Processes wait indefinitely for resources held by one another.

Starvation: A philosopher may keep waiting while others repeatedly get a chance to eat. This basic solution does not guarantee fairness.
